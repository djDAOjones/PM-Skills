## Pass 1 — audit (six findings)

1. **The fig transplants but never prunes.** Every mechanism paid
   per-item rent, yet the incumbent's net surface *grew* through
   the burst (new template, new prompt, three changelog archives,
   organs added, two sentences deleted). The rent rule has no net
   ledger; 4.x now carries both all its ceremony and the new
   machinery, while the successor evidence says much of the
   mechanics-prose is frontier-dead.
2. **Velocity outran evidence diversity.** ~26 items in days,
   single tier, zero external data — magnificent on the fitness
   function's numerator, unverified on its real denominator
   (consuming projects). The only brakes that worked were the
   gates and this reflection.
3. **The transcript corpus never materialised** (firing-rate
   probe). TRANSCRIPT-SHA shipped in 4.2.0; the close reminds
   every task; zero transcripts saved since. Meanwhile every
   harness scenario to date used synthetic seeds successfully —
   the corpus theory never earned use, and native session storage
   covers recall.
4. **Janitor-report commit churn.** Committing the regenerated
   report on every close writes SHA/timestamp noise into history
   that nothing has ever read back; freshness is a filesystem
   property, not a git one.
5. **Shared-append residue is the last structural conflict
   point.** RQ7 proved records dissolved everything except
   decision-log/trajectory appends, where a hand-union damaged
   seams a reviewer had to catch. The fix candidates are parked
   but unowned.
6. **The regression net was built and never hung.** No harness
   scenario has run at a release since R0 — including 4.5.0, which
   restructured the very changelog the upgrade scenario exists to
   protect. The advisory eval line was designed and never wired
   into the release close.
